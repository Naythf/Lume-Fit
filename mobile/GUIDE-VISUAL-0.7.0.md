# Meu guia visual · 0.7.0

Closes #82. App only; published website, native origin, private account scope, backup bridge and database schema remain unchanged. Replaces the text-heavy 0.6 summary rather than adding another layer over it.

## What changes

- The first screen goes straight to energy, protein and drinks, with brief context beside the quantities. BMI and target difference move to a secondary disclosure. Methods open on request; there is no Sources/Limits wall or external link in the guide or its explanations. Formal credits and research remain in this document and the app's About section as plain attribution.
- Energy works at 18. The old UI accepted age 18 while the old energy function required 19, leading to an unexplained “Não estimada”. An unspecified physiological reference now produces the two-reference range instead of silently removing the estimate. Missing inputs and clinical/age/measurement boundaries produce a specific reason and one-step edit action.
- Explicit Maintain/Gain/Reduce scenarios use maintenance or a modest ±10% starting scenario. They do not infer a weight-change deadline. The interface labels estimates, and offers the calculation on demand. Reduction is disabled below conservative thresholds; maintenance remains visible. Changes are private and do not modify the profile's recorded weight or create diary entries.
- Protein shows grams/day and the weight-based calculation. Day-to-day uses the adult population reference; regular-training mode shows a range. A direct switch avoids repeating the whole questionnaire. A chart represents the selected reference, not measured intake. Actual diary values, with coverage, can be compared when entered.
- Drinks have an initial numeric reference and manual +250 mL/undo tracking. The glass animates from the previous recorded fraction to the next. It does not infer hydration from photos, count water in food as drinks, or claim everyone needs the same amount. Unknown diary values remain unknown, not consumed zero.
- Twelve food combinations now include ingredient grams, approximate household measures, kcal, protein, carbohydrate and fat. The 0.5–2× portion control recalculates all quantities. The private day draft replaces another selection of the same meal type, totals only the compatible choices, and never logs food as consumed. Generic examples can be read without optional health disclosure; use in a personal draft requires confirmed context/restrictions. Known exclusions apply even while browsing generic examples. Ingredients with listed allergens are labelled; contamination and preparation differences still require review.
- Energy-density examples compare the same 100 g, with bars proportional to tabulated kcal. The calculator divides the energy and mass of the same entered portion.
- The generated plate replaces the schematic diamonds. It is explicitly illustrative; its pixels and apparent food volume are not calculation inputs. The reused gauge disappears from the weight empty state. Private-photo notices use a short 12 px footnote. Existing weight/photos/notes/edit/delete/gallery remain intact.
- One SVG chevron replaces the inherited border-chevron plus a rotated “+”. Data edits keep keyboard focus and avoid replaying the whole page entrance. Graph/reveal motion follows LumeMotionPreference; reduced motion retains the final values and geometry.

## Calculation methods and limits

For adults who explicitly confirm a general context, energy uses Mifflin-St Jeor resting expenditure, `10×kg + 6.25×cm − 5×age`, then the selected published offset (−161 or +5) and an approximate activity multiplier (1.2/1.375/1.55/1.725). With neither reference chosen, both endpoints are shown. Endpoints are rounded to 50 kcal. The original 1990 study sampled ages 19–78; this educational product accepts adults from 18 and treats that one-year extension as an approximation, not an independently validated age-specific model. The feature's upper bound remains 78; BMI outside 18.5–40 routes energy to individual assessment. BMI itself remains descriptive, not a diagnosis.

Gain/reduce are explicit illustrative scenarios of +10%/−10%, not values asserted to be individually optimal. A reduction is not applied with BMI below 20 or a resulting low endpoint below 1200 kcal for the selected female reference, or 1500 otherwise. Those are conservative product guardrails, not universal minimum-energy prescriptions. Contexts such as pregnancy, clinical treatment and distress with weight/calorie tracking do not receive calculated personal targets.

Protein: EFSA population reference 0.83 g/kg/day for healthy adults. Training: ISSN's 1.4–2.0 g/kg/day range for most exercising adults; 1.6 g/kg is a selected point within that range for the visual comparison and macro example, not a statement that each person needs exactly that number. Energy allocation uses the calculated protein, up to 30% fat, and carbohydrate remainder (at least 45%); if a compatible positive allocation is not possible, no misleading breakdown is drawn. Percentages and 4/4/9 kcal factors are kept in full precision for chart geometry; displayed grams are rounded. Tabulated food energy need not exactly equal a simplified rounded 4/4/9 reconstruction because food composition tables use their own factors and rounding.

Water: EFSA total-water references of 2.0/2.5 L/day under moderate conditions include food. The drinks starting reference is approximately 80% of those totals, 1.6/2.0 L/day; without a chosen reference the range is shown. The glass uses the center for visual comparison when a range exists. Heat, exercise, food moisture and clinical instructions change needs. There is no automatic fixed addition for sweating. The tracking ceiling of 10,000 mL is an input/data limit, not recommended intake.

Food values: TACO/NEPA-Unicamp 2011, small prepared-food subset, with original row IDs in nutrition.js. Quantities use edible and prepared weights as named. Water contributes volume/mass and zero energy. Recipe values are ingredient sums, not photo estimates. Extra oil, sauces, brands, losses and unlisted ingredients are not silently inferred. Household measures are approximate. Diet pattern, known allergens and time filter the examples; another unlisted restriction does not receive an assertion that a meal is safe. The catalog is not proof of micronutrient adequacy; exclusively plant-based nutrition includes a brief B12 attention note.
